# Artist Website — Future Sensibilities Breadcrumb

Purpose: preserve the strategic lessons drawn from established national/international gallery- and museum-level artist websites for future iterations of spencertracybrown.com.

## Core orientation

The site should present a **practice**, not a brand.

Spencer's work does not depend on repeating the same visual answer. Coherence should come from recurring inquiry, not forced stylistic sameness. The site should make the same mind legible across different media.

Current conceptual through-line:

**body → boundary → intrusion → protection → connection → transformation**

Related recurring concerns:
- embodiment
- thresholds
- interiority
- material evidence / memory
- constructed identity
- spatial transformation
- relation and obstruction
- making as thinking

## Lessons from established artist websites

### 1. Let the work carry weight

Do not over-explain every image. Strong artist sites give artworks visual space and assume they deserve attention.

Current rule:
- artwork title
- short descriptor where useful
- avoid paragraph-length interpretation beneath every image

As bodies of work mature, move explanation upward to the **series/project level** instead of repeating interpretation per work.

### 2. Evolve from medium taxonomy toward bodies of work

Current medium categories remain useful scaffolding:
- Sculpture & Installation
- Ceramics & Material Studies
- Drawing, Painting & Mixed Media
- Object & Creative Fabrication

But the likely mature architecture is organized by **investigation / body of work**, not by medium.

Possible future structure:
- Boundaries
- Bodies & Transformation
- Constructed Beings
- Objects & Vessels
- Spatial / Fabrication Work

This transition should happen only when enough strong work exists to justify each body of work. Do not manufacture series to satisfy navigation.

### 3. Range does not require visual sameness

Multiple media can coexist if conceptual recurrence is strong enough. The site should not apologize for breadth or try to make sculpture, ceramics, painting, fabrication, and environments look artificially alike.

Medium is evidence. Inquiry is identity.

### 4. Build an archive without flattening hierarchy

Older work, Tucson studio history, ASU-era work, process images, and supporting pieces can remain visible.

The solution is not deletion; it is hierarchy:
- **Selected Work** = strongest current evidence
- **Series / Projects** = deeper bodies of work
- **Archive** = historical continuity and breadth

Old work is not inherently dilution. Uncurated work is.

### 5. Selected Work should become increasingly selective

As the body grows, the front-facing portfolio should tighten rather than expand indefinitely.

Target mature front-facing set: roughly **10–15 strongest works**, with additional material deeper in the archive.

Selection standard:
- strongest conceptual and material resolution
- good documentation
- contributes to recurring inquiry
- earns its place rather than merely proving versatility

### 6. Major works and series should eventually receive their own pages

Candidates as the bodies mature:
- Boundaries
- Pandemic / interior-pressure figures
- Constructed-being lineage around *Minerva Beta Form*
- Suspended / threshold / spatial work

A series page can hold:
- selected works
- concise series statement
- installation views
- process where meaningful
- chronology

This is useful for both human viewers and search indexing, but the primary reason is artistic coherence.

### 7. Keep more context than blue-chip sites need

Established artists can rely on galleries, museums, press, Wikipedia, auction records, and decades of external authority. Spencer cannot yet assume that context exists elsewhere.

Therefore the site should remain somewhat more explanatory for now:
- clear artist statement
- concise biography / profile
- CV
- press / independent record
- titled works
- useful short descriptors

Reduce explanation only as external recognition and bodies of work become dense enough to carry it.

## Spencer-specific guardrails

- Do **not** optimize toward "content creator" behavior.
- Do **not** create work merely to feed a posting schedule.
- Documentation is a **record of practice**, not algorithmic feed-fodder.
- Do **not** force a commercial signature motif.
- Wheel-thrown ceramic objects are the closest natural repeatable formal language; repetition there comes from format and craft, not from repeating the same answer.
- Preserve experimentation and mutation across media.
- Avoid language that turns Spencer into a "brand." Prefer: practice, body of work, inquiry, visual language, archive, series, project, record.

## Current strategic conclusion

The website architecture is increasingly sufficient. The next major leap should come primarily from:

1. stronger new work
2. better documentation
3. deeper series
4. ruthless selection
5. gradually reorganizing from medium categories toward bodies of inquiry when the evidence supports it

Do not redesign merely to simulate maturity that the work itself should provide.

## Trigger for future reconsideration

Revisit the site architecture when at least two of these are true:
- Boundaries has 3+ resolved works
- Pandemic/body-transformation lineage has expanded beyond literal Covid subject matter
- Constructed-being lineage has 2–3 resolved works beyond *Minerva Beta Form*
- Suspended/spatial work has 2+ strong documented pieces
- front-facing Selected Work exceeds ~15 genuinely strong pieces

At that point, consider a series/project-based information architecture while preserving the current visual restraint.
